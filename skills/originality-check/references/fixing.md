# Fixing originality findings

## Matched passage from a source
Pick one:
1. **Quote** when the exact wording matters (a definition, a legal clause, a striking finding): put it in quotation marks, cite it with the page or section, and keep it short (one or two sentences). Quotes should be rare.
2. **Paraphrase properly** when the idea matters: close the source, write the point from memory in a new structure (different sentence order, different emphasis, the student's own example or number), then check the source again for accuracy and cite it. Swapping synonyms in the same sentence structure is still plagiarism.
3. **Summarise** when only the conclusion matters: one sentence in the student's words plus the citation.
4. **Cut** when the passage does not earn its place.

After rewriting, run the `humaniser` skill (academic mode) on the paragraph so the new text reads like the rest of the student's writing.

## Matched passage from another project (copy case)
Rewrite from the student's own material: their organisation, their data, their survey results, their reasoning. If the overlap is a framework explanation (for example what PESTEL is), cut the explanation; examiners do not need it. If two students genuinely share a data source, each must present and interpret it independently.

## Quotation without a citation
Add the citation (author, year, page or section). If the source cannot be found, remove the quotation marks and rewrite as the student's own claim only if it is genuinely their own; otherwise cut it.

## Numbers and ideas
Numbers, findings and distinctive ideas need a citation even when the wording is original. Common knowledge (for example "India's financial year ends in March") does not.

## What never counts as a fix
- Inventing, guessing or misattributing a citation.
- Inserting typos, invisible characters, homoglyphs or odd spacing to defeat a scanner (the clean-user-facing-text skill removes these, and scanners detect them).
- Machine "spinning" of sentences with synonyms.
- Moving copied text into images or tables.
