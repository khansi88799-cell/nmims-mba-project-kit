# The three NMIMS formats: templates and marking rubrics

Each rubric has 10 parameters worth 10 marks each (100 total). Map every parameter to a chapter and make the evidence for it easy to find. The chapter skeletons in `../formats/` already follow these templates.

## Problem-Solving Proposal (managerial: real-world problem solving)
**Needs a specific organisation or department.** If the student has no access to an employer case, use a company with public evidence (annual reports, regulatory filings, published policies) as the case organisation, and say so. A listed company works well because its numbers, risks and policies are public.

| Template section | Chapter |
|---|---|
| 1 Title page | Cover |
| 2 Executive summary: problem, analysis, solution, impact | 1 |
| 3 Introduction: background of the organisation; nature and scope of the problem | 2 |
| 4 Problem identification and situational analysis: core problem; SWOT, PESTEL; stakeholder mapping | 3 |
| 5 Solution development: alternatives; final solution; justification with data/theory | 4 |
| 6 Implementation plan: steps, timeline, resources, responsibilities | 5 |
| 7 Business/organisational impact: financial, operational, strategic; sustainability and risk | 6 |
| 8 Reflection and learning: leadership, teamwork, decision-making | 7 |
| 9 References; 10 Appendices | 8, 9 |

Rubric: (1) clarity of problem definition; (2) depth of situational analysis (SWOT, PESTEL, root cause); (3) stakeholder analysis; (4) innovative and practical solutions; (5) analytical rigour and use of frameworks; (6) evaluation of alternatives and trade-offs; (7) implementation plan; (8) impact assessment (incl. sustainability, scalability); (9) leadership and team collaboration; (10) presentation and communication.

## Business Plan (entrepreneurial)
| Template section | Chapter |
|---|---|
| 1 Cover page: business name, student, SAP ID, specialisation | Cover |
| 2 Executive summary: idea, opportunity, value, goals | 1 |
| 3 Market opportunity: size, trends, segmentation; competition | 2 |
| 4 Business concept: UVP; product/service | 3 |
| 5 Business model: revenue streams, cost structure, partners/resources | 4 |
| 6 Marketing and go-to-market: promotion, pricing, distribution, positioning | 5 |
| 7 Financial plan: projections, break-even, capital needs; estimated P&L, cash flow, balance sheet | 6 |
| 8 Risk analysis and mitigation | 7 |
| 9 Implementation plan: timeline, operations, hiring, logistics | 8 |
| 10 Sustainability and social impact | 9 |
| 11 References; 12 Appendices | 10, 11 |

Rubric: (1) market opportunity analysis; (2) innovativeness and uniqueness; (3) feasibility study (technical, operational, economic; realistic resource forecasts); (4) business model viability (scalability); (5) financial planning and projections (all three statements, break-even, investment needs); (6) risk assessment and mitigation; (7) marketing and go-to-market; (8) operational execution plan; (9) sustainability and social impact; (10) presentation and communication.

## Research Project (academic)
| Template section | Chapter |
|---|---|
| 1 Title page | Cover |
| 2 Abstract | 1 |
| 3 Introduction: background, significance; problem and objectives | 2 |
| 4 Literature review: theoretical framework; gaps | 3 |
| 5 Methodology: design; data sources, sampling, tools | 4 |
| 6 Data analysis and findings | 5 |
| 7 Discussion: comparison with previous studies; implications | 6 |
| 8 Conclusion: insights; recommendations | 7 |
| 9 Limitations and future research | 8 |
| 10 References (APA/MLA); 11 Appendices (survey tools, raw data) | 9, 10 |

Rubric: (1) clarity of research problem and objectives; (2) literature review and theoretical framework; (3) research design and methodology; (4) data quality and relevance (incl. ethics); (5) analytical rigour; (6) critical thinking and insight; (7) originality and contribution; (8) implications and recommendations; (9) limitations and future research; (10) presentation and documentation.

**Research needs real primary data** (survey or interviews). Plan it in Semester III; it cannot be faked or skipped.

## Choosing a format (when the student is unsure)
- Has an employer/department with a real problem and some data: Problem-Solving.
- Has a venture idea and wants to build it: Business Plan (but if the title was already registered as Problem-Solving, anchor the idea in a named organisation's problem and present the venture as the proposed solution).
- Wants to test a question with data, has 3-4 months for a survey: Research.
